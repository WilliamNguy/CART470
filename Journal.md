# week 3

## Overview

  This week we met up with our client, Florian. We were given more context to our project scope, and the vision our client wants this project to become. We realized how much freedom we have to make this prototype come to life. As we disccussed the boundaries of this project, we found multiple issues that we would encounter in the future. The main issue isn't implementing the 360s or the point click features into the web-browser but using the 36-channel sound device and putting it into our code. Florian did mention alot about the fact that these high qualities sounds contains large gigabytes of data. This would be a challenge to implement into our web-browser. There are a few techniques we could look into to help us sail past these obstacles: Splitting the audio files into smaller multi-channel files, compressing the audio, or finding a system that can properly process these sounds through the browser.

After the meeting, we started researching different ways we could approach this problem. One option we found was JSAmbisonics, which is made for playing and processing Ambisonic audio through a web browser. This could be useful for our project because it can take multi-channel spatial audio and turn it into a binaural experience for headphones. It also allows the sound field to rotate, meaning we could potentially connect the movement of the 360 image with the direction of the audio. For example, if the user turns around in the 360 environment, the sound would also change based on where they are looking.

We also looked into IAMF and Eclipsa, which Florian mentioned during our meeting. These could potentially help us with compressing and playing the immersive audio without having to load the original massive files directly into the browser. For now, I think our main focus should be testing these different technologies with a small audio sample before working with the full 36-channel recordings. Once we know that we can get the spatial audio working properly in the browser, we can start connecting it to the 360 environment and eventually the interactive Concordia map.

Overall, this meeting helped us understand that the sound is really the main focus of the project. The map and 360 images are there to help navigate and visualize the different locations, but the goal is to create an experience where sound is the primary way of experiencing the space. Our next step is mainly technical research and creating a simple prototype to see what is possible before moving onto the more complex parts of the project.
