# week 3

## Overview

  This week we met up with our client, Florian. We were given more context to our project scope, and the vision our client wants this project to become. We realized how much freedom we have to make this prototype come to life. As we disccussed the boundaries of this project, we found multiple issues that we would encounter in the future. The main issue isn't implementing the 360s or the point click features into the web-browser but using the 36-channel sound device and putting it into our code. Florian did mention alot about the fact that these high qualities sounds contains large gigabytes of data. This would be a challenge to implement into our web-browser. There are a few techniques we could look into to help us sail past these obstacles: Splitting the audio files into smaller multi-channel files, compressing the audio, or finding a system that can properly process these sounds through the browser.

After the meeting, we started researching different ways we could approach this problem. One option we found was JSAmbisonics, which is made for playing and processing Ambisonic audio through a web browser. This could be useful for our project because it can take multi-channel spatial audio and turn it into a binaural experience for headphones. It also allows the sound field to rotate, meaning we could potentially connect the movement of the 360 image with the direction of the audio. For example, if the user turns around in the 360 environment, the sound would also change based on where they are looking.

We also looked into IAMF and Eclipsa, which Florian mentioned during our meeting. These could potentially help us with compressing and playing the immersive audio without having to load the original massive files directly into the browser. For now, I think our main focus should be testing these different technologies with a small audio sample before working with the full 36-channel recordings. Once we know that we can get the spatial audio working properly in the browser, we can start connecting it to the 360 environment and eventually the interactive Concordia map.

Overall, this meeting helped us understand that the sound is really the main focus of the project. The map and 360 images are there to help navigate and visualize the different locations, but the goal is to create an experience where sound is the primary way of experiencing the space. Our next step is mainly technical research and creating a simple prototype to see what is possible before moving onto the more complex parts of the project.

# Week 4

## Overview

This week we met up with the TA for our weekly meeting and explained to her what our plan was and what we had done so far. At this point we did not really have a prototype yet, since last week was mainly focused on research. We each gathered our own research on different libarries and ways we could approach the project, and then tried to implement what we found into a more concrete work plan.

At the end of the class, we had the chance to catch Florian and have a little chat with him about the project. He provided us with a phone that we could use to start recording sounds. The reason for using this specific phone was because it already had a paid sound recording app installed on it that we could use. Since we now had something to record with, we decided to leave class a little early and actually start collecting material around the campus. We recorded four different locations, three of them being around the main intersections of Concordia and another one inside the metro. Alongside the audio recordings, we also took panoramic pictures at each location so that later we can match the visual environment with its sound.

We also started organizing the technical side of the project by making a pipeline in Figma. The pipeline helped us visualize how everything could eventually connect together. We separated the project into things such as Figma, HTML/CSS/JavaScript, the audio recordings, panoramic photographs, JSON data, database, storage and potentially Python. The idea is that each location would eventually have its own information such as the GPS, time, date and ID, which would then connect the right panoramic image with the right sound recording. From there the user could select a location from the map and enter its soundscape.

Daniel also managed to make our first working prototype. For now it is still very experimental and uses test files, but he managed to integrate a 360 panoramic image inside the browser along with a working multichannel 3D audio player. The prototype lets us upload a panoramic image and a multichannel audio file, and then move around the 360 environment. The audio is also spatial, so the goal is that the sound changes based on the direction the user is looking. This was important because it showed us that the main idea of combining the 360 image with immersive audio can actually work in the browser.

The next step is to start replacing the test material with the recordings and panoramic pictures we collected around Concordia and see how well they work inside the prototype. We also still need to figure out the best way to organize and store all the recordings and images, and how we will connect everything to the map. The prototype is still very early, but now we have something
